# No undo

## Campaign

Offcut is a fictional independent print studio. Invite curious beginners to a social two-colour printmaking evening: make a small edition of ten prints, with paper, ink and a first drink included. The action is to reserve a place. All sender, venue, date and offer details are sample content.

## Visual idea

A two-ink print that has escaped its registration marks. Make the email feel like a contemporary workshop poster held in your hands: warm stock, forceful cobalt typography, vermilion interruptions, tactile cut paper and a real brayer. A short and confident composition, with a strong asymmetric art/type relationship and a ticket-like booking close. No stock hero, card grid or long programme stack.

The headline is NO UNDO. The supporting voice is specific: Your hands. Two inks. Ten prints. Perfect edges? Not tonight. The image is a graphic editorial still life of the actual tools and printed shapes, with no lettering baked into the artwork.

Palette: warm yellow paper #f3ebcd, cobalt #1838d0, vermilion #e34428, dark ink #20272c. Oversized heavy sans-serif title with a narrow, practical monospace voice for workshop details. The live typography and the inky physical artwork must carry equal weight.

On mobile, the oversized words remain the opening event. Artwork gets a clear full-width moment, and the booking details keep a short readable sequence. Preserve strong colour fields, generous type scale, the image/type contrast and a visible booking action.

## Concept study

Create a full-email visual study before examining the Weavour schema. Keep this study separate from production image assets. The generated study may include illustrative typography; every essential word will be live editable content in the implementation.

## Translation decisions

The implementation uses a 440px-wide poster canvas and native Arial Black title typography, Impact supporting headings and system fallbacks. The two title lines use different live type sizes so the idea remains oversized at narrow widths. The study’s distressed lettering becomes crisp editable text; the tactile surface stays in the original photograph. The artwork is a standalone image, and its side-note becomes a separate live caption. The booking close uses short paired details and a clear live button. The secondary headline was shortened to “Perfect edges? Not tonight.” after inspecting mobile wrapping. No essential copy is baked into an image.

A fallback-font check at 320px caught overflow in the first condensed title. The final title uses heavy Arial Black with a bold Arial fallback and sizes chosen to fit both families at 320px. This preserves the large two-line title without relying on an installed condensed font. The vermilion strip is darkened to #c3321f to keep its live cream text legible.
